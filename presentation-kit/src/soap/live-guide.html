THE SOAP COMPUTER · SPEAKER NOTES · ABOUT 3 MINUTES

Local laws. No global oracle.

  1. Predict. “Six pins between two plates. Will one dip always give the shortest connected network?” Take a prediction. Press Reveal one dip. Balanced junctions look persuasive; ask whether that is enough to certify the answer.
  2. Intervene. Press Next, then Compare five-side network. Count the five unit edges. The displayed length is computed from the pins, so the comparison is checkable. Press Try a second dip: same pins and numerical rule, another start. The overlay clears so the new network is visible on its own. Read the actual lengths. These are two selected, reproducible runs, not a success-rate study.
  3. Reveal. Press Next. Rotate the tetrahedron. “How many sheets share a line? How many triple lines meet at a point?” Press Reveal the junctions. Bright lines: three sheets at 120°. Gold point: four triple lines at the tetrahedral angle, arccos(−1/3), about 109.47°. These are Plateau’s junction laws.
  4. Establish. Press Next. Jean Taylor’s 1976 theorem gives ideal area-minimizing films in three-dimensional space only three interior local types: smooth sheet, Y, T. Switch the frame. Local rules constrain the geometry; they do not guarantee that each real dip or numerical run finds a global optimum.

Keep the claims separate

Theorem: Taylor’s interior regularity/classification result, away from the wire boundary; technically, two-dimensional Almgren minimal sets in ℝ³, locally equivalent to the plane, Y or T minimal cones. Numerics: the existing local network solver and 600 steps of the existing area-relaxation solver. The 3D mesh connections are prescribed. No global optimality or convergence certification is claimed. Rendering: the thickness field and colours illustrate the geometry; this browser, solver and picture are not formally verified.

Controls and recovery

← / → change scenes. R or Reset scene restores the current scene, including its camera and intervention state. Buttons work by touch and keyboard. Drag 3D films to rotate; wheel to zoom. N opens cues; F requests fullscreen. Views are static until changed. If WebGL2 is unavailable, a visible error replaces the 3D demonstration; do not describe an unrendered scene as tested.
