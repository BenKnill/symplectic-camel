  1. Observe. “Which dents suggest a swirl?” Watch Ben’s recovered, normal-speed boat clip from 1 June 2026. Compare with the labelled illustration, reveal its spins, then view from above. The clip observes a surface; the model offers a mechanism, not a reconstruction.
  2. Pressure. “Double circulation: double depth?” Set Γ to 40, then 80. The displayed dip quadruples with fixed core radius. Pressure supplies the inward force. The depth profile is exaggerated; the formula is an idealization, not a river measurement.
  3. Circulation. “What does a loop around opposite spins measure?” Select the opposite pair; centre x = 0, radius = 3. Signed contributions cancel. Moving water need not give nonzero net circulation. This frozen measuring contour is not Kelvin’s moving material loop.
  4. One tube. “Same spin at both ends?” Switch closed ring → surface arch. Follow the arrows: opposite surface senses can belong to one tube. Real attachment involves deformation and reconnection. This is possible geometry, not a diagnosis of every dimple.
  5. Visibility. “If the mark vanishes, has the vortex gone?” Sweep reflection position; the dashed depression stays fixed. Contrast changes without changing the dip. Separate real 3D stretching from the ideal flat model’s area preservation.
  6. Evidence. “Can one dimple measure local downwelling?” Compare 2023 simulated regional counts with 2026 laboratory dimple-and-scar area coverage. These are different statistical observables. The June preprint reports nonlocal links. A clue is not a calibrated local meter.

Land this: Surface marks suggest hidden motion, but a model and independent evidence are needed to interpret them. The recovered field clips show actual surface observations. The separate illustrations do not reconstruct the Rhine or determine oxygen transfer. No theorem or formal verification of the renderer is claimed.
