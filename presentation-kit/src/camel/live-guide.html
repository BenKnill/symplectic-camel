SPEAKER NOTES · ABOUT TWO MINUTES · FIVE BEATS

Try to beat the obstruction.

  1. Predict · 20 seconds. The ball is four-dimensional; the missing coordinate is colour. The cylinder has a smaller circular base and two unlimited directions. Ask: “If we preserve volume, can the ball still fit?” Wait for a prediction.
  2. Intervene · 20 seconds. Next shrinks the first two coordinates and expands the other two. The ball fits while four-volume stays constant. Point to the shadow inside the blue ring. Volume alone does not prevent squeezing.
  3. Change the rule · 25 seconds. Next preserves each conjugate area. One coordinate shrinks, its partner expands. The ellipse keeps its area and its long direction sticks out. The linear map’s containment is analytic.
  4. Try a nonlinear escape · 25 seconds. Next, then Play. Exact mathematical kicks, drifts and rotations are symplectic. Bending still cannot put the whole ball in the smaller conjugate cylinder: that is Gromov’s theorem. Pause before moving on.
  5. Reveal the distinction · 25 seconds. Next changes the cylinder base to two position coordinates. Both may shrink while their momenta expand. The ball now fits. Land: “Which two coordinates make the hole matters.”

What this establishes

Symplectic geometry imposes a stronger condition than volume preservation. Gromov’s non-squeezing theorem concerns the whole ball and a conjugate-plane cylinder. The screen is a numerical illustration, not a proof of the theorem.
